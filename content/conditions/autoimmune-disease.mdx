## Support the person, not just the diagnosis

Autoimmune diseases are a broad group of conditions in which the immune system reacts against the body’s own tissues. Rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, Graves’ disease, type 1 diabetes, multiple sclerosis, psoriasis, Crohn’s disease, and ulcerative colitis are not interchangeable—and they do not share one universal treatment.

I work alongside a patient’s medical team. I do not advise stopping prescribed medication, and I do not present food, supplements, or gut care as a guaranteed cure.

## What I review

- Your confirmed diagnosis, current symptoms, and specialist recommendations
- Medications, supplements, allergies, and possible interactions
- Digestive symptoms, appetite, and nutrient intake
- Sleep quality, stress load, movement, smoking, and alcohol use
- Objective laboratory results when they are relevant to care
- Which changes are realistic enough to maintain

## The gut–immune connection: what it means

The immune system and intestinal environment communicate in complex ways, and researchers continue to study how the microbiome relates to autoimmune disease. An association does not prove that every autoimmune condition begins in the gut or that changing the microbiome will reverse it.

Digestive symptoms still deserve proper evaluation. If celiac disease, inflammatory bowel disease, infection, or a nutrient deficiency is suspected, the next step should be condition-specific testing—not a generic “detox.”

## A supportive plan may include

### Food adequacy before restriction

I look for a balanced pattern with enough protein, fiber, healthy fats, and micronutrients. Restrictive diets can create deficiencies or make eating unnecessarily stressful, so elimination diets should have a clear goal, time limit, and reintroduction plan.

### Recovery and daily rhythm

Sleep, appropriate physical activity, stress-management skills, and smoking cessation can support general health and quality of life. They complement medical care; they do not replace it.

### Careful supplement decisions

“Natural” does not mean risk-free. Some herbs and nutrients can interact with anticoagulants, immune-modifying drugs, thyroid medication, or diabetes treatment. I review purpose, dose, and monitoring before recommending them.

## My goal for you

My aim is to help you reduce confusion, identify modifiable factors, and build habits that support your overall health while respecting the treatment plan for your specific autoimmune condition.
