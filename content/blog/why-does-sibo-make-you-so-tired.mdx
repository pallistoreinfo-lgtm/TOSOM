SIBO makes you tired by impairing nutrient absorption, increasing inflammation, and disrupting gut-brain energy balance.

There are many side effects of Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (SIBO) associated with **SIBO**. One condition patients suffering from SIBO  frequently complain of is unexplained fatigue.

Most people associate **SIBO with digestive discomfort** like bloating or stomach pain. Many people are not aware that it can drain your energy significantly.

Over the years, I’ve treated countless patients struggling with this **mysterious fatigue**, and here’s what I’ve discovered.

### **The Connection Between Gut and Energy**

In my clinical experience, your gut plays a powerful role in how energized—or drained—you feel each day.

When your **digestive system** isn’t functioning properly, your body can’t absorb essential nutrients like vitamin B12, iron, and magnesium—nutrients your cells need to produce energy.

Some of my patients eat super healthy and still feel wiped out. The missing link? Their gut isn’t absorbing what their body needs.

### **6 Reasons Why Does SIBO Make You So Tired**

![Why Does SIBO Make You So Tired](/public/assets/images/2025/04/Why-Does-SIBO-Make-You-So-Tired.jpg)

After working with many **SIBO patients**, I’ve identified some of the main reasons why this condition leads to fatigue:

#### **Nutrient Malabsorption**

One of the first things I check for is **nutrient deficiencies**. To do this, I don’t guess, I test my patients with a micronutrient blood test. The bacteria in the small intestine often compete for nutrients, particularly iron, vitamin B12, vitamin D, and folic acid.

These deficiencies can leave you feeling exhausted and weak, no matter how much rest you get.

#### **Increased Inflammation**

**Chronic inflammation** is another common thread in **SIBO cases**. Inflammation activates the **immune system** and releases cytokines—proteins that regulate inflammation but also make you feel fatigued.

Your body behaves as though it’s fighting a persistent, low-level infection around the clock.

#### **Disrupted Sleep Patterns**

Many of my patients tell me they have trouble sleeping due to **bloating** or abdominal discomfort, especially at night. Poor sleep reduces your body’s ability to recharge, compounding the exhaustion.

#### **Increased Toxin Production**

Harmful bacteria don’t just steal your nutrients—they also produce toxins. When these toxins enter your bloodstream, they can cause what we call “**systemic toxicity**,” which contributes to sluggishness and that foggy-headed feeling.

SIBO doesn’t just affect digestion—it can throw your hormones out of balance, too. I often see patients suffering from **SIBO** **with** **imbalances in cortisol**, **serotonin, and melatonin**—hormones that influence mood, energy, and sleep. When these are out of sync, fatigue follows.

#### **Immune System Burden**

Finally, SIBO places a continuous burden on the immune system. Your body is constantly working to control the overgrowth of harmful bacteria, and over time, this depletes energy reserves.
